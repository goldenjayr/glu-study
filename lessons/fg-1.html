Introduction to the Gospels

Four portraits of one Lord — synoptics, reliability, and how each Gospel answers who Jesus is.

Summary

Four portraits of one Lord: historical Gospels with OT narrative shape, synoptic overlap, and clear answers to who Jesus is — Messianic King, Suffering Conqueror, Compassionate Savior, Eternal Son.

Big ideas

What to teach and remember

  • Gospels follow OT historical-narrative pattern (with some Greco-Roman biography features); generally chronological, sometimes topical or geographic.
  • Audience: the Christian community. Cultural soil: OT Jewish culture.
  • Reliability rests on theological conviction, corroboration, the Spirit, candid disciple failures, and distinct access (Matthew/John eyewitnesses; Mark with Peter; Luke with Paul and others).
  • Synoptics = Matthew, Mark, Luke (“seen together”); majority view is Markan priority. John often includes what they omit.
  • Who Jesus is: Matthew — Messianic King; Mark — Suffering Conqueror; Luke — Compassionate Savior; John — Eternal Son of God.
  • Follow Him: from the heart; willing to suffer; with compassion; loved by God and loving others.
  • True King: power over demons, authority to forgive, power to heal and raise. Kurios = Lord; euangelion = good news; kingdom language outweighs “gospel/evangelize.”
  • Early church received the four as genuine; Eusebius: John approved the synoptics — the only indisputable ones.
Key terms

People, places, and words that matter

Synoptic

“Seen together” — Matthew, Mark, Luke cover mostly the same material, often with the same words.

Kurios

Greek for Lord — the title that names Jesus’ authority.

Euangelion

Greek for good news — the message of the kingdom and the King.

Markan priority

Majority theory: Mark written first; Matthew and Luke used Mark.

Josephus

Jewish historian who mentions Jesus — extra-biblical corroboration.

Talmud

Jewish writings that mention Jesus — further historical witness.

Side-by-side contrasts

Who Jesus is — four Gospels

  • Matthew — Messianic King

  • Luke — Compassionate Savior
  • John — Eternal Son of God

How we follow Him

  • Matthew — from the heart, not just outwardly
  • Mark — willing to suffer for Him
  • Luke — with compassion on the needy
  • John — loved by God and loving others

Story style (Matthew vs Mark)

  • Matthew — shorter stories but more of them / compendium

For the pulpit

Sermon hooks

  • Four cameras, one Lord — preach the portrait that fits today’s text without flattening the others.
  • Kingdom language outweighs “gospel” talk in the four books — let people hear the King’s reign.
  • Reliability is pastoral: candid failures, eyewitness access, and Spirit-guided record of the real Jesus.
