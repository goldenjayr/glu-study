Key terms

People, places, and words that matter

Revelation

The act whereby God gives us knowledge about Himself that we could not know otherwise; the truth basis for arguing that the Bible is the Word of God.

Inspiration / theopneustos

The Holy Spirit’s guidance of the human writers so they communicated God’s message accurately, while still using their own personalities and styles. The Greek idea of God-breathed (theopneustos) belongs here — not to the word Inerrancy.

Inerrancy

What God revealed and inspired is true, reliable, authoritative, and without error. The word does NOT come from the Greek for God-breathed.

Dual authorship

Scripture’s human and divine authorship together; the argument for the unity of Scripture is based on this dual authorship.

Pentateuch / JEPD

Genesis through Deuteronomy. This course rejects the claim that the Pentateuch was written by four or more writers (JEPD-style multi-writer theory).

Fulfilled prophecy

Empirical evidence that Scripture’s message is from God. Enemies of Scripture do not always attack the facts of fulfilled prophecy.

Compare

Side-by-side contrasts

Revelation vs Inspiration vs Inerrancy

Inerrancy ≠ theopneustos (God-breathed)

  • God-breathed / theopneustos belongs to inspiration — the Spirit’s work through the writers

Essay themes

If you preach or review

Revelation / Inspiration / Inerrancy

Revelation is God's act of making known what we could not otherwise know about Himself. Inspiration is the Holy Spirit's guidance of the human writers so they communicated God's message accurately, while still using their own personalities and styles. Inerrancy means that what God revealed and inspired is true, reliable, authoritative, and without error. In short, revelation is the giving of the truth, inspiration is the Spirit's work through the writers, and inerrancy describes the resulting Scripture.

Eight arguments / evidences Scripture is God’s Word

1. The unique revelation of the person of Christ.
2. The Bible's extraordinary claim that its message is from God.
3. The empirical evidence of fulfilled prophecy.
4. The convicting, convincing, and converting power of its message.
5. The inexhaustible infinity of revelation.
6. The unity of the message from many human sources.
7. The transcultural appeal of the message.
8. The unmistakable honesty of the Scriptures.

Four aspects of inspiration

1. Inspired men: holy men of God were moved, or carried along, by the Holy Spirit, so they wrote what God wanted communicated.
2. Inspired personalities: God used each writer's personality, background, vocabulary, and writing style rather than erasing the human author.
3. Inspired words: the very words of Scripture were directed by the Holy Spirit, not merely the general ideas.
4. Inspired thoughts and message: the content, thoughts, and ideas came from God, making Scripture completely accurate, authoritative, and without error.
