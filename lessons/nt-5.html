The Gospel According to John

Holy of Holies of the four Gospels — believe, and live.

Big ideas

What to teach and remember

  • Philip Schaff called John the Holy of Holies of the four Gospels — the greatest of the four, the golden sunset of inspiration. Preach it with that reverence: this is not a fourth rerun of the same story; it is the inner room.
  • The author is John son of Zebedee, the Beloved Disciple. Written late, about 90–100 AD, traditionally from Ephesus. The Rylands Fragment (about 135 AD) already carries this Gospel into the next generation — the book was traveling while the church was young.
  • John 20:30–31 is the purpose sentence and a preaching commission: these signs are written so that you may believe Jesus is the Christ, the Son of God, and that by believing you may have life. The two key words are believe and life — not grace and faith as a pair.
  • Seven I Ams (bread of life; light of the world; the door; the good shepherd; the resurrection and the life; the way, the truth, and the life; the true vine) and seven miracles plus one more in chapter 21. The number 7 signals completeness and perfection of Christ’s revelation — that is the pastoral takeaway, not a hunt for mysticism.
  • About 90% of John is new beside the Synoptics. He omits genealogy, birth, temptation, transfiguration, parables, the ascension, and the Great Commission. He adds the prologue, Lazarus, Peter restored, and the Spirit discourses. Four portraits, one Lord — do not mash them into a blur.
  • Jesus cleansed the Temple at the beginning and at the end of His public ministry. John 2:20 (“forty-six years this temple has been in building”) points toward about 26 AD for that first cleansing — history sitting inside the sign.
  • Nicodemus must be born anothen — from above. “Water and the Spirit” is Hebrew parallelism: natural birth and spiritual birth, not baptismal regeneration. Do not preach John 3 as a requirement to get wet in order to be saved.

1. According to Phillip Schaff, the church considers John the greatest of the four Gospels.

TrueSchaff called it the Holy of Holies, the golden sunset of inspiration.

2. John was known as the 'Beloved Disciple.'

TrueJohn son of Zebedee is the disciple Jesus loved. That is how the church has known him.

3. The two key words in John are 'grace' and 'faith.'

FalseThe two key words are believe and life, not grace and faith.

4. John emphasizes the full deity of Jesus.

TrueFrom the prologue onward: the Word was God. John will not let you keep a merely human Jesus.

5. John's use of the number 7, suggests his strong commitment to Jewish mysticism.

TrueMoodle marks this True. Jay-R missed it (9/10). The workbook is ambiguous here; 7 also simply signals completeness and perfection of Christ’s revelation. Review the miss — do not treat mysticism as the pastoral takeaway.

6. Approximately 90% of John's material is supplemental to the Synoptics.

TrueNearly all of John is new beside Matthew, Mark, and Luke. Read it as a complement, not a rerun.

7. All of John's recorded miracles are found in the other Gospels..

FalseMost of John’s signs are his own. Lazarus, Cana, and the rest are not a Synoptic reprint.

8. Tradition suggests that John's Gospel was penned in Ephesus.

TrueTradition places the writing in Ephesus, late in the first century.

9. Jesus cleansed the Temple both at the beginning and the end of His public ministry.

TrueJohn opens with a cleansing; the Synoptics close with one. Two visits, one holy zeal.

10. To say that one must be born of water and the Spirit means that one must be baptized in order to be saved.

FalseAnothen means from above. Water and Spirit is Hebrew parallelism (natural vs spiritual birth), not baptismal regeneration.
Essay themes

If you preach or review

The seven I Am statements

I am the bread of life; the light of the world; the door; the good shepherd; the resurrection and the life; the way, the truth, and the life; the true vine. Each is a sermon in a sentence — hunger, darkness, belonging, care, death, lostness, fruitlessness — and Christ names Himself as the answer. Preach them as revelation, not as slogans.

John beside the Synoptics

About 90% of John is supplemental. He omits genealogy, birth, temptation, transfiguration, parables, the ascension, and the Great Commission. He adds the Word-made-flesh prologue, Lazarus raised, Peter restored, and the long Spirit discourses. Four portraits, one Lord — do not flatten them.

Insights from Nicodemus

Anothen means from above (and again). Water and Spirit is Hebrew parallelism: the birth that is of flesh and the birth that is of the Spirit, not a formula that baptism regenerates. Nicodemus comes at night with questions; Jesus answers with new birth. Preach the conversation, not a sacrament as the savior.
