Renaissance and Reformation — Luther, Zwingli, Calvin, and Church Music

Italy’s Renaissance; Luther’s 1517 challenge to indulgences; Zwingli in Zurich; Calvin’s Christian society and Servetus; congregational singing.

CompleteQuiz 100%
At a glance

Summary

The Renaissance began in Italy. The Reformation was sparked by Luther’s opposition to the sale of indulgences (Ninety-Five Theses, 1517). Luther was Augustinian, not Dominican. Calvin’s Institutes were not published in 1517. Zwingli led in Zurich, taught a symbolic Lord’s Supper, wanted civil government under Christ, and died in battle in 1531. Calvin sought a Christian society, valued music (did not disdain it), and was responsible for Servetus’s burning. Isaac Watts later championed congregational singing.

Essay themes

If you preach or review

Summarize the life of Martin Luther

Martin Luther (1483–1546) was an Augustinian monk and teacher in Wittenberg. Through studying Galatians and Romans, he came to emphasize salvation by grace through faith in Jesus Christ rather than indulgences or the merits of the church. In 1517 he posted his Ninety-Five Theses, challenging the sale of indulgences and sparking the Protestant Reformation. At the Diet of Worms he stood by his conscience and the Scriptures, and Frederick protected him afterward. Luther translated the Bible into German and helped bring the Word of God to ordinary people. His teaching and example shaped Protestant Christianity.

Summarize the impact of Ulrich Zwingli on the Reformation

Ulrich Zwingli was the Swiss preacher in Zurich who helped lead the Reformation alongside Luther. He opposed the traditional fast during Lent and other practices that lacked scriptural support, and he taught that the Bible—not the Pope or church government—was the sole religious authority. He supported civil government being submitted to Christ and helped establish Reformed churches in Zurich and other Swiss cities. He taught that the Lord’s Supper was symbolic rather than the actual physical presence of Christ in the elements. His influence spread the Reformation through Switzerland until his death in battle in 1531.

What do we know about church music during the Reformation?

Church music became an important part of the Reformation because the reformers believed the whole congregation should praise God, not only a professional choir or priest. Luther encouraged singing in the language of the people and wrote German chorales to teach biblical truth and doctrine. Calvin emphasized the Psalter, with congregational singing generally in unison. Protestant worship music therefore included Lutheran chorales and Calvinist psalm singing, while instruments were used less directly during congregational hymn singing. The result was active participation by ordinary believers in worship.
