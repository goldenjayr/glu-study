Thyatira: The Medieval Church (800–1517)

Thyatira the Medieval Church (800–1517) — Middle Ages from Rome’s fall (476); East/West split not immediately after the First Crusade; minimized Bible preaching (poor priest training); congregational singing not critically important (yet Francis emphasized it); Waldo, Wycliffe, Huss, and Savonarola as Reformation forerunners; by 1500 four of five original Christian stronghold cities under Islam.

At a glance

Summary

Lesson 6 covers Thyatira — the Medieval Church (800–1517). The East/West split did not happen immediately after the First Crusade. Many date the Middle Ages from Rome’s fall in 476 and the collapse of the Roman Empire. Medieval preaching of the Bible was minimized because priests were poorly trained, and congregational singing was not a critically important part of worship — though Francis of Assisi did emphasize it. Peter Waldo was forbidden to preach by the Pope; John Wycliffe lived to see the Lollard revival; John Huss was burned for preaching the Bible; Savonarola was not burned like Huss. By 1500, four of the five original stronghold cities of Christianity were under Islam.

Essay themes

If you preach or review

Describe the development of worship in the Roman Catholic Church during in Medieval times.

Worship began with the simple services of the early Christians but gradually became a complex ceremony led by priests. Bible preaching was minimized because many priests were poorly trained, and services were conducted in Latin rather than the language of the people. Communion became mainly a priestly function; only the officiating priest received the bread and cup, and the emphasis was on Christ's death rather than the resurrection and Second Coming. Prayers increasingly asked God to receive help from the saints, while congregational singing was nearly eliminated. Gregorian chant, cantillation, antiphons, and hymns remained, but ordinary believers had little active participation.

Describe the attempts to revive the Roman Catholic Church through the monasteries and religious orders?

The Benedictine reform at Cluny was a major attempt to renew the Catholic Church. In 909 Duke William of Aquitaine gave Abbot Berno a charter for a monastery at Cluny that was free from outside control. Other Benedictine monasteries were reorganized on this model, and Cluny became known for financial integrity, discipline, and social reform. Francis of Assisi also sought renewal after his conversion, abandoning a pleasure-seeking life and embracing poverty and the gospel. The Franciscan order was formally established in 1210, and its brothers preached widely, even beyond Italy. These communities brought spiritual seriousness, reform, and gospel preaching back into the church.

Who were Waldo, Wycliffe, Hus, and Savonarola? In what ways did they prepare the church for the coming Reformation?

Peter Waldo was a wealthy merchant who gave up much of his wealth, wanted to preach the gospel, and helped form the Waldensian movement. The Waldensians valued the Bible in the people's own language, lay preaching, and the priesthood of all believers. John Wycliffe called the English church back to its biblical foundations, translated the Scriptures into English, and sent the Lollards to preach among the villages. John Hus preached a similar message in Bohemia, led reform, and was martyred for Christ. Girolamo Savonarola preached boldly against corruption in Florence, challenged both church and political leaders, and was eventually executed. Together they kept Scripture, gospel preaching, vernacular Bible translation, lay ministry, and moral reform before the church. Their witness exposed the need for renewal and prepared the way for the Reformation.
