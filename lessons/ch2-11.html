Essay themes

If you preach or review

1949 Los Angeles Crusade

The 1949 Los Angeles Crusade began through the invitation of Christ for Greater Los Angeles, a fellowship of about 200 churches. Billy Graham approached it with urgency, believing that revival in Los Angeles could have repercussions around the world. He asked that all churches be included, the budget be raised from $7,000 to $25,000, and a tent seating 5,000 be built. Stuart Hamblin, a popular radio personality, urged listeners to attend and was later converted, which Graham viewed as God's sign to continue. William Randolph Hearst then ordered his newspapers to ‘Puff Graham,’ and the media attention expanded the meetings. Jim Vaus, a wire-tapper for the Mob, was also converted. The meetings continued for 72 nights, thousands professed faith, and 82 percent had never made a profession before. The crusade launched Graham's worldwide citywide evangelistic ministry.

Hebrides Revival

The Hebrides Islands had declined spiritually after World War II; no church could point to a single young person attending, while young people went to dances, films, and drinking houses. Peggy Smith, an 84-year-old blind prayer warrior, and her sister Christine prayed for revival. Peggy's vision of crowded churches was confirmed by Pastor James Murray MacKay, who led his leaders in prayer for three months. A young deacon's question, ‘O God, are my hands clean? Is my heart pure?’ was followed by deep conviction. MacKay invited Duncan Campbell, who first declined but later came when circumstances changed. Campbell's preaching brought a solemn awareness of God's presence, repentance, and salvation. More than 600 gathered outside one church, and the revival spread rapidly to neighboring districts. People prayed through the night, homes experienced salvation, and drinking houses closed for a generation. The revival showed me the importance of persistent, honest prayer and repentance.

Chinese Organic House Church / underground church

The Chinese Organic House Church, or underground church, developed almost everywhere in China but is difficult to locate because it has no public structure. Estimates place the movement at about 130 million believers, alongside roughly 23.5 million registered members of the official Three-Self Church. It grew without church buildings, programs, denominational support, foreign missionaries, or a formal organizational network. When the Communists took power, missionaries were expelled, pastors and believers were imprisoned, and many outward professors abandoned the faith. Yet the gospel continued to spread through believers meeting in homes, apartments, restaurants, and other ordinary places. Its strength came from committed disciples, prayer, witness, and dependence on God rather than buildings or institutions. For me as a pastor, this movement is a reminder that the church is people gathered in Christ and that persecution cannot extinguish the true light of the gospel.
