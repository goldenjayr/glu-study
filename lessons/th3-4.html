Lesson 4 · Course 57

What We Believe About Sin

The fall, imputed sin, and the three lusts — and why sin’s ruin meets the gospel’s remedy.

CompleteQuiz 90%
At a glance

Summary

Adam was not unclear about God’s expectation. Eve was tempted in the lust of the flesh, eyes, and pride of life. The fall affected more than Adam and Eve alone; God is not powerless for allowing sin. All are involved with sin nature, imputation, and personal sin (Moodle: False that some only have nature/imputation without personal sin). Sin is act or attitude; Adam’s disobedience imputes sin, Christ’s death imputes righteousness to believers; sin’s influence reaches every part of human nature.

Big ideas

What to teach and remember

  • Adam was not unclear what God expected — that is not why he sinned.
  • Eve was tempted in lust of the flesh, lust of the eyes, and pride of life.
  • The garden was harmonious until they sinned; the fall did not affect only them.
  • God was not shown to be less than all-powerful by allowing sin.
  • Q6 miss: from God’s perspective stem about penalty evidenced by “cruse of Christ” is False (Moodle).
  • False that some have nature + imputation but never commit personal sin.
  • Sin can be defined as an act or an attitude.
  • In Adam sin is imputed; in Christ righteousness is imputed to believers.
  • Sin’s influence extends to every part of man’s nature.
  • Gospel hope restores spiritually and promises resurrection.
Key terms

People, places, and words that matter

Lust of the flesh

Good bodily desire turned against God’s command; remedy: self-control and submission.

Lust of the eyes

Attraction/ambition turned to coveting or control; remedy: contentment and obedience.

Pride of life

Self as center, wanting to be like God; remedy: humility and dependence on grace.

Imputed sin

In Adam’s disobedience, sin is imputed to all people.

Imputed righteousness

In Christ’s death, righteousness is imputed to all who believe.

Sin nature

All are born with a sin nature; Moodle rejects that some never commit personal sin.

Compare

Side-by-side contrasts

In Adam vs in Christ (imputation)

  • Adam’s disobedience: sin imputed to all
  • Christ’s death: righteousness imputed to believers

Three lusts — desire, result, remedy

  • Flesh → self-gratification → self-control / submission
  • Eyes → coveting/control → contentment / obedience
  • Pride of life → self-rule / alienation → humility / grace

Essay themes

If you preach or review

How we can see sin in ordinary human life

We can see sin in ordinary human life: everyone faces physical death, and people experience suffering and sickness. Even babies show an unlearned selfish desire. People are inclined to lie and can be motivated to harm one another. Sin also shows itself in guilt and shame, alienation from God, and a depraved desire that draws us toward further sin.

Physical and spiritual consequences of the fall

The fall brought physical and spiritual consequences to both Adam and Eve. Physically, death entered, the man would labor over a cursed ground and rule the creation with difficulty, while the woman would experience multiplied sorrow and pain in childbirth and a troubled relationship with her husband. Spiritually, both felt guilt and shame, were alienated from God, lost their positive inclination toward Him, and developed a depraved desire that drew them toward sin. The earth and animal world were also affected by divine judgment. The hope is God's grace and the gospel, which restore the spiritual condition and ultimately promise resurrection.

Lust of the flesh, lust of the eyes, and pride of life

The three expressions are the lust of the flesh, the lust of the eyes, and the pride of life. The lust of the flesh is a good bodily desire turned against God's command; its result is sinful self-gratification, and it is answered by self-control and submission to God's will. The lust of the eyes turns attraction and ambition into coveting, power-seeking, or control; its remedy is contentment, disciplined desire, and obedience. The pride of life makes the self the center, seeking independence and wanting to be like God; its result is self-rule and alienation, and its remedy is humility, dependence on God, and trust in His grace. Ultimately, the gospel is God's remedy for the ruin caused by sin.
