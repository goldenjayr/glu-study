Lesson 3 · Course 52

Accuracy and inspiration of Scripture

God-breathed words, inerrancy, faulty theories, and a closed canon.

CompleteQuiz 100%
At a glance

Summary

God-breathed words, inerrancy, faulty theories, and a closed canon. Inspiration is the Spirit’s superintending work so that writers wrote what God wanted, without error. Theopneustos in 2 Timothy 3:16 means God-breathed — life in the words.

CompleteQuiz 100%
Big ideas

What to teach and remember

  • Inspiration is the Spirit’s superintending work so that writers wrote what God wanted, without error. Theopneustos in 2 Timothy 3:16 means God-breathed — life in the words.
  • Four qualities to keep straight: inspired men (borne along, 2 Peter 1:21), inspired writers (God used personality and style), inspired words (verbal, not ideas only), inspired results (inerrancy and authority).
  • Name the faulty views so you can smell them in a conversation: intuition, conceptual, illumination-only, partial, limited (“only the religious bits”), dictation, and KJV-only inspiration of a translation.
  • We hold inerrancy because Scripture claims it, Jesus treated it as final truth (“It is written”), and God cannot lie. Reliability is also backed by history, archaeology, and prophecy.
  • The autographs are gone; thousands of manuscripts remain. Textual criticism, rightly used, aims to recover the original wording — not to unseat the Word.

The Bible’s own arguments for inerrancy

Self-witness, the character of God, and the Lord’s own use of Scripture. We do not prop the Book up with mood; it claims what it is.

Quiz drill
Quiz drill / review · open to practice · 10 items

Question wording reconstructed from session notes.

Quiz score: 100% · 1 attempt

1. The word theopneustos appears in 2 Timothy 3:16.

TrueGod-breathed. That one word carries a pulpit’s confidence.

2. The author was guided to write what God wanted.

TrueSuperintendence, not religious guesswork.

3. God gave the thoughts but not the words.

FalseVerbal inspiration: the words matter, not only the vibe.

4. Conceptual inspiration means God inspired the ideas.

TrueThat is a real view — and a faulty one. Ideas without words will not hold a church.

5. Limited inspiration means Scripture is authoritative only in religious dogma.

TrueGeorge Eldon Ladd’s kind of limit. It carves the Bible into “safe” and “skippable.”

6. Some dismiss passages as morally objectionable.

TruePeople still edit God. The pastor’s job is not to help them.

7. The prophets never claimed to speak the words of God.

FalseThey claimed it constantly. That is why “thus saith the Lord” lands with weight.

8. Jesus appealed to the authority of the Word as final truth.

True“It is written” is how the Lord Himself argued.

9. Parts of Daniel are in Chaldean.

TrueThe Book is at home in history and language, not in a fog.

10. Textual criticism aims to recover the wording of the autographs.

TrueA servant task: get back to what was written, not replace it.
