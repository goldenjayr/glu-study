Structure and Strategy in Acts

Six geographic expansion periods — internal growth and external opposition — and Luke as a reliable, selective, teaching narrator.

At a glance

Summary

Acts Lesson 2 traces six geographical periods of expansion. Internal growth/tension and external growth/opposition reinforce each other. Luke writes a reliable, selective, episodic narrative that teaches implicitly. Pentecost is unique equipping; historical details are corroborated. Watch the misses: property-sharing context in other Acts homes; narrative percentage ~70% not 90%; apostleship limited to NT apostles who saw the risen Lord with a direct calling.

Big ideas

What to teach and remember

  • Six geographical periods of expansion structure Acts movement.
  • Internal growth/tension and external growth/opposition reinforce one another.
  • Luke = reliable, selective, episodic, implicitly teaching narrative.
  • Pentecost is special/unique equipping.
  • Historical details in Acts are corroborated.
  • Miss 2.3 Q1: other Acts accounts show believers meeting in wealthy homes without sharing resources (not merely a persecution/famine exception).
  • Cumul miss Q14: narrative percentage is 70%, not 90%.
  • Cumul miss Q15: apostleship = only NT apostles who saw the risen Lord + direct calling (not a broader modern apostleship).
  • Acts 1:8 order: Jerusalem → Judea → Samaria → ends of the earth; six geographical periods divided by summaries.

For the pulpit

Sermon hooks

  • Map the six geographies so Acts does not feel like random travel stories.
  • Preach both insides and outsides of growth — tension and opposition belong in the advance.
  • Luke selects episodes that teach; preach the implicit lesson, not only the plot.
  • Keep apostleship tethered to the risen Lord eyewitnesses and calling.
