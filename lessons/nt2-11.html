Big ideas

What to teach and remember

  • John writes as one who knew Jesus intimately — heard, seen, and handled Him — at a time when he appears to have been the only surviving apostle.
  • The key word in 1 John is not “Believe.” Moodle marked that claim False. The letter’s burden is fellowship, walking in the light, and confessing that Christ has come in the flesh.
  • 1 John combats Gnostic/Docetic heresy: teachers who treated matter as evil, denied that Christ truly came in the flesh, claimed special knowledge, and separated from the church.
  • In 1 John, sin is treated as a child’s offense against his father: do not practice sin, do not sin, know we are sinners, confess, and receive the Father’s immediate forgiveness and cleansing.
  • A person can know they are saved in more than two ways. The foundation for true doctrine is a proper understanding of Jesus Christ.
  • 3 John condemns those who destroy the faith with struggles for supremacy in the church and earthly pride.
  • Jude’s message is a warning against immorality and apostasy — not directed only to believers in Jerusalem. The letter begins and ends with assurance.

Essay themes

If you preach or review

Heresy combated by 1 John (Gnostic/Docetic)

1 John combats early Gnostic teaching — the claim that spirit is good but matter is evil, and that Christ did not truly come in the flesh. Some denied the reality of Jesus’ body (Docetism); others denied His true humanity; others separated the divine Christ from the human Jesus. These teachers claimed special knowledge and separated from the church. John answers with apostolic eyewitness: Jesus was heard, seen, and handled. Believers must confess that Jesus Christ has come in the flesh. He calls the church to walk in the light, in righteousness, in love, and in fellowship, so that those who believe can know they have eternal life and live in victorious fellowship with God.

Five ways 1 John treats sin as a child's offense against his father

1 John treats sin as a child’s offense against his father in five ways: (1) He expects the believer not to practice sin — whoever has been born of God does not live in a settled pattern of sin (1 John 3:9). (2) He expects us not to sin: “My little children, these things I write to you, that you may not sin” (2:1). (3) He knows we are sinners, both by nature and by sinful acts; denying this deceives us and means God’s Word is not in us (1:8, 10). (4) He expects us to confess our sins (1:9). (5) When we walk in the light and confess, God immediately forgives and cleanses our sin through the blood of Jesus Christ (1:7, 9).

Seven ways/proofs of assurance of salvation

1 John gives seven proofs that can give a person confidence they are saved: (1) Have a Savior — having the Son means having life; without the Son there is no life (1 John 5:12). (2) Have the witness of God, especially the testimony God has given of His Son (5:9-10). (3) Walk in the light: a saved person turns from darkness and lives honestly before God (1:7). (4) Obey God’s commandments: keeping His word shows that we know Him (2:3-5). (5) Love other Christians: love for the brethren is evidence that we have passed from death to life (3:14). (6) Believe that Jesus is the Christ and that He came in the flesh; faith in the Son gives eternal life (4:2; 5:1, 12). (7) Have the Spirit’s witness and confidence in prayer: the Holy Spirit confirms that we belong to God, and we can come to Him knowing He hears us (3:24; 5:14-15). These proofs are not a claim of being perfect. They give a believer confidence while he continues to walk with Christ.
