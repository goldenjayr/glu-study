Smyrna & Pergamos: The Persecuted and Worldly Church (100–800)

Smyrna the Persecuted Church (100–313) and Pergamos the Worldly Church (313–800) — John the last Apostle (~100), early learning and worship, Justin Martyr’s order of worship, Ambrose’s doctrinally pure hymns, desert refuge under persecution (to Diocletian), Jesus’ word to Pergamos (Balaam/Nicolaitans), Augustine (Confessions & City of God; not first to use Trinity), and Patrick of Ireland.

Summary

By 150 the Bible is not yet translated into German. Augustine was not the first theologian to use the term Trinity. In Justin Martyr’s early order of worship, the cup was not reserved only for presiding ministers. Ambrose of Milan wrote doctrinally pure hymns. Under Roman persecution the desert became a refuge for many seeking inner peace; persecution peaked under Diocletian. Patrick is the patron saint of Ireland.

Key terms

People, places, and words that matter

Smyrna

The Persecuted Church epoch (100–313) — era of Roman persecution after the Apostolic age; refusal to sacrifice to Roman gods / emperor as divine fed hostility.

Pergamos

The Worldly / State Church epoch (313–800). Jesus commended holding fast His name where Satan’s throne was, yet rebuked Balaam and Nicolaitan tolerance.

John (last Apostle)

Around AD 100, John — the last surviving Apostle — dies (Quiz 5 True).

Justin Martyr

Early Christian apologist whose order of worship shows bread and cup given to worshippers — not cup only to ministers.

Ambrose of Milan

Known for writing doctrinally pure hymns; his influence and Scripture reading helped lead Augustine to conversion.

Desert refuge

Under Roman persecution, the desert became home for many searching for inner peace.

Diocletian

Emperor under whom official persecution of the church reached its most severe form (essay on persecution 100–313).

Balaam / Nicolaitans

False teachings Jesus rebuked at Pergamos — led toward idolatry, compromise, and immoral practices; He warned He would fight them with the sword of His mouth.

Augustine of Hippo

North African bishop (baptized 387); Confessions and The City of God; NOT the first to use the term Trinity; shaped church teaching for centuries.

Patrick

Patron saint of Ireland (Quiz 5 True).

For the pulpit

Sermon hooks

  • Smyrna: the church was hated for confessing Jesus as Lord instead of the emperor — faithfulness under pressure shaped identity.
  • Pergamos: hold fast His name where Satan’s throne is, yet repent of Balaam/Nicolaitan compromise — Jesus fights false teachers with the sword of His mouth.
  • Worship that forms disciples: learning, shared table (Justin), and doctrinally pure song (Ambrose) — not spectacle.

Essay themes

If you preach or review

Explain why the church was severely persecuted between 100 and 313 AD.

The church was persecuted for several reasons. Christians refused to sacrifice to the Roman gods or honor the emperor as divine, and they confessed Jesus as Lord, which made Rome see them as a rival and an unlawful society. Their separation from pagan entertainment and practices also made them seem antisocial. Because believers met at night and called one another brothers and sisters, false rumors accused them of immorality, cannibalism, and other crimes. Many Christians came from the poor and dispossessed classes, so critics dismissed Christianity as a movement of the ignorant. Their faithful lives also convicted pagans of sin. These pressures, along with official hostility and repeated imperial decrees, led to persecution that reached its most severe official form under Diocletian.

Explain what Jesus said to the church at Pergamos.

Jesus commended the church at Pergamos for holding fast to His name even though it lived where Satan's throne was and where Antipas had been killed. However, He rebuked the church for tolerating people who followed the teaching of Balaam and the Nicolaitans. Those teachings led believers toward idolatry, compromise, and immoral practices. Jesus therefore called them to repent and warned that He would fight against the false teachers with the sword of His mouth. He promised hidden manna, a white stone, and a new name to those who overcame.

Who was Augustine of Hippo and why was he important?

Augustine of Hippo was a North African Christian leader, born to a Christian mother and a pagan father. He was highly educated in philosophy and rhetoric and taught rhetoric in Carthage, Rome, and Milan. The influence of Ambrose of Milan and his reading of Scripture led to his conversion; he was baptized in 387. He later became a priest and then bishop. Augustine was important because his Confessions and The City of God shaped Christian thinking and helped establish the direction of church teaching and practice for centuries. His writings also gave the church a deep testimony of grace and conversion.
