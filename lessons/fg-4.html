Luke

Jesus the Savior — reversal for Gentiles, sinners, women, and the poor.

At a glance

Summary

Luke presents Jesus as the compassionate Savior for a wider Gentile Christian audience — orderly history for Theophilus, salvation as reversal and deliverance, with special attention to the poor, sinners, Gentiles, and women.

Big ideas

What to teach and remember

  • Luke: Paul’s partner and physician — not a Jew, not an apostle — writing orderly history for Theophilus and a wider Gentile Christian audience.
  • Date window: after Mark (~AD 65), before about 67; comparisons show Luke used Mark.
  • Structure tracks geography and salvation history: Galilee → journey to Jerusalem → ministry in/near Jerusalem → crucifixion and resurrection; smaller-scale guideline is geography.
  • Salvation in this lesson: primarily reversing people’s conditions (least/last/lost become first/most/found); deliverance from tyranny of evil and God’s judgment against sin; often called “necessary.”
  • Surprising saved: the poor, sinners, Gentiles, and women.
  • Temple cleansing: they were leaving no room for the Gentiles — that most upset Jesus in this lesson.
  • Miracles show He is the Messiah; baptized to identify with sinful humans as Savior.
  • Review misses: Gentiles crowded out at the temple; salvation as reversal (not “forgiveness” as the primary word here); deliverance from evil and judgment (not only reconciliation).

What salvation is (this lesson)

For the pulpit

Sermon hooks

  • Preach the reversal: least become first — Luke’s Savior finds the lost without flattering the powerful.
  • Temple courts matter: when worship crowds out the nations, Jesus overturns tables.
  • “Necessary” is pastoral courage — the cross was not Plan B.
