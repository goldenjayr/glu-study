Big ideas

What to teach and remember

  • Trials build faith, reveal weaknesses, correct sin or wrong direction, and prepare believers for God’s work.
  • Loneliness is unwanted isolation. Solitude is chosen time with God.
  • Scripture, effective prayer, Christian friends, and meaningful relationships help us through difficulty.
  • Usually the deeper the winter’s night, the greater the preparation for future usefulness. God allows frustration and pain to move us closer to Him.
  • Most of God’s great servants went through great trials — including Paul. Before God uses people in a mighty way, He often allows a long, dark, lonely night to build mighty faith.
  • A winter’s night may come from limited understanding, unconfessed sin, a wrong direction, or a need to learn patience for a new vision.

Essay themes

If you preach or review

What prompts a winter's night? Discuss the four answers.

1) Limited understanding — God teaches truth for a greater task. 2) Sin that needs confession and correction. 3) Wrong direction — see it and turn back. 4) Need to learn patience and wait for a new vision. These times prepare; they are not meant to destroy.

What is the prescription to get through a dark night?

Know the spiritual purpose of the frustration; do not let it destroy self-worth. Use loneliness to draw closer to Christ. Look for what God wants to give. Let your loneliness become ministry to others with the same problem. Study biblical relationships, pray for a meaningful friendship, turn negative thoughts inside out, do something useful, and add friends to your prayer list.

What do we learn from those who failed their time of testing?

Samson was blind to what God was doing. Others gave in to evil desire. Demas loved this present world and lost sight of God’s purpose. Saul would not wait or obey — he intruded into the priest’s office and later gave incomplete obedience concerning Amalek. Stay humble, resist temptation, keep God’s purpose in view, and obey fully.
