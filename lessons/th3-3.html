Lesson 3 · Course 57

Looking at Two Extremes

Calvinism and Arminianism — TULIP, conditional salvation, and how Dort framed the debate.

CompleteQuiz 100%
At a glance

Summary

Calvinism’s essential belief is summarized by TULIP; Arminians hold conditional salvation. The image of God grounds moral choice—repenting, turning, and believing. Total depravity means inability to please God. “Elect” refers to the corporate church or all believers. Dort condemned Arminius for conditional salvation (among other charges). Arminius taught a person becomes a sinner only when consciously and voluntarily sinning; salvation can be lost if the believer denies Calvary; confusing salvation and sanctification yields a “second work of grace.” Regeneration is God’s work.

Big ideas

What to teach and remember

  • Calvinism acronym: TULIP.
  • Arminian belief about salvation: conditional salvation.
  • Image of God → moral choice (repent, turn, believe).
  • Total depravity = inability to please God.
  • Elect = corporate church / all believers.
  • Dort condemned Arminius for conditional salvation (plus experiential neglect of Calvary and wrong perfection).
  • Arminius: man becomes a sinner only when he consciously and voluntarily sins.
  • Arminian loss of salvation: if the believer denies the work of Calvary.
  • Salvation/Sanctification confusion = second work of grace.
  • Regeneration is God’s work.

Compare

Side-by-side contrasts

Calvinism vs Arminianism (as framed in this lesson)

  • Calvinism: TULIP
  • Arminian: conditional salvation; sinner only when consciously/voluntarily sins
  • Loss: if believer denies Calvary; second work of grace when salvation/sanctification confused

Dort’s charges against Arminius

  • Conditional salvation
  • Experiential focus neglecting Calvary’s non-experiential accomplishment
  • Wrong interpretation of perfection
For the pulpit

Sermon hooks

  • Name the extremes without caricaturing people — TULIP and conditional salvation are maps, not insults.
  • Preach moral choice under the image of God: repent, turn, believe.
  • Hold regeneration as God’s work even while calling sinners to respond.
  • Don’t confuse being saved with growing holy — “second work” talk often mixes salvation and sanctification.
