Essay themes

If you preach or review

How Paul discipled Timothy

Paul found Timothy in Derbe and Lystra — a young man with a good testimony among the believers. His mother Eunice was a Jewess who believed, and his father was a Greek. Grandmother Lois and mother Eunice had taught him the Holy Scriptures and given him a sincere faith. Timothy had also seen Paul suffer when Paul was stoned at Lystra. After John Mark left, Paul invited Timothy to travel and minister with him. Paul had Timothy circumcised, not to save him or force a Jewish identity, but so the Jews would not be offended and Timothy could minister among them. Paul gave him the decrees from the Jerusalem church and trained him by serving beside him. Timothy became Paul’s son in the faith and a trusted helper. Through his help the churches were strengthened in the faith and increased in number.

Ten principles on how to be led of the Spirit (Acts 16:6-11)

The ten principles in Acts 16:6–11 are these: (1) Make a plan to carry out God’s work, but be willing for the Spirit to change it. (2) Have a clear goal of what you want to accomplish — Paul wanted to preach the Word. (3) Have a strategy. Paul planted churches in large cities, looked for places with a Jewish population, and looked for responsive people first. (4) Follow both open doors and closed doors. God can lead by giving an opportunity or by forbidding a way. (5) When the way is not clear, obey the last clear direction and wait on God instead of forcing a door. (6) Go as far as you can go. Paul continued to Troas, the farthest point he could reach. (7) God may lead by an unusual event or vision. Paul received the vision of the Macedonian man. (8) Take others with you for support. Luke, Timothy, Silas, and Paul went together, and the passage changes to “we.” (9) Hindsight helps us put the pieces together and understand God’s plan. (10) Circumstances can confirm the Spirit’s leading. The quick, straight trip to Neapolis and the events that followed confirmed the direction. Being led by the Spirit includes prayerful planning, obedience, patience, and checking our direction against God’s work.

Paul’s experience in Athens (Acts 17:15-34)

Paul’s experience in Athens teaches us to begin with people where they are. Athens was full of idols, and the people liked to discuss new ideas. Paul was troubled by the idols, but he still spoke respectfully. He reasoned with the Jews and Gentile worshipers in the synagogue, then spoke daily in the marketplace. He used the altar to the unknown God as a point of contact. He did not approve their idols; he used what they knew to explain the true God who made the world and is Lord of heaven and earth. He taught that God is near, that people must turn to Him, and that God will judge the world through the Man He raised from the dead. When Paul mentioned the resurrection, some mocked him, but others believed. This is contextualization: making contact with unsaved people and helping them give an honest hearing to the Gospel. Meet people where they are and lift them to the truth. Paul did not change the Gospel even though some called his approach a compromise.
