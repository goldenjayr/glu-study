What Is Apologetics and What Is Its Purpose

A reasoned defense of the faith — and an aid against doubt.

CompleteQuiz 100%
At a glance

Summary

Apologetics is a reasoned defense of Christianity and an aid to faith against doubt. Left unanswered, challenges embolden scoffers. This lesson maps four functions of apologetics, Van Til’s presuppositional approach, and why evolutionist talking-points are not raw “facts.”

Big ideas

What to teach and remember

  • Apologetics = reasoned defense of the Christian faith and an aid to faith against doubt.
  • Unanswered challenges bolster the pride of scoffers and critics — silence is not neutral.
  • Four essential functions: positive confirmation, defense, refutation, and application/persuasion.
  • Presuppositional apologetics (Van Til): expose non-Christian principles by reducing them to absurdity.
  • Ken Ham is a well-known young-earth creationism advocate.
  • Do not treat evolution claims as observed facts; natural selection is not molecules-to-man evolution.
  • John Warwick Montgomery is not the one who, “in his poetic way,” defended the faith (Moodle: False).
  • “Apologia” does not occur 57 times in the New Testament (Moodle: False).

For the pulpit

Sermon hooks

Essay themes

If you preach or review

List and discuss the four functions of Apologetics.

I understand the four functions this way:

1. Positive confirmation: apologetics marshals philosophical, scientific, historical, and other evidence to make a positive case that Christianity is true and reasonable.

2. Defense: it answers objections, criticisms, and questions raised against Christianity, explaining and clarifying the Christian position.

3. Refutation: it exposes weaknesses in non-Christian religions and philosophies and answers the arguments offered for them. Refuting another view by itself does not prove Christianity, so this belongs alongside the positive case.

4. Application and persuasion: apologetics seeks more than intellectual agreement; it persuades people to receive and apply the truth of Christianity in life, serving the gospel and ministry.

List and briefly discuss the seven aims of Apologetics as related to ministry.

The seven ministry-related aims are:

1. To prepare a person for evangelistic ministry.
2. To provide an aid to faith when doubt arises.
3. To minister to the unsaved by answering honest intellectual and spiritual questions.
4. To prepare the ground for sowing the seed of God's Word.
5. To strengthen, confirm, and establish the faith of believers.
6. To prepare the mind of the average unsaved person and motivate him or her to search for the truth.
7. To set forth the reasonableness and coherence of the Christian system to the world.

List the ten arguments that evolutionists should avoid and explain why they should be avoided.

The ten arguments to avoid are:

1. "Evolution is a fact." It is a theory about the unobserved past, built on assumptions, not a directly observed fact.
2. "Only the educated reject evolution." This simply dismisses opponents by labeling them uneducated; many well-educated scholars reject it.
3. "Overwhelming evidence in science supports evolution." Evidence is interpreted through prior presuppositions; it does not settle historical origins by itself.
4. "Doubting evolution is like doubting gravity." Gravity can be observed and tested repeatedly, while molecules-to-man evolution cannot be tested in the same way.
5. "Doubting evolution is like believing the earth is flat." The earth's shape is directly observable; evolutionary stories about fossils and common descent are not direct observations.
6. "The evidence is clear, so it must have evolved." A conclusion cannot prove that the assumptions or premises behind it are true.
7. "Natural selection is evolution." Natural selection works on existing characteristics; it does not demonstrate that a lower life-form became a fundamentally higher one.
8. "Common design means common ancestry." Similar structures can reflect a common Designer just as well as, or better than, an unobserved common descent.
9. "Sedimentary layers show millions of years of deposition." Layers show that sedimentary layers exist; the millions-of-years interpretation depends on an assumed geological history.
10. "Mutations drive evolution." Mutations corrupt existing genetic information; they do not supply the kind of new information required for molecules-to-man evolution.

The common problem is treating an interpretation of evidence as if it were an observed, proven history, while overlooking the role of presuppositions.
