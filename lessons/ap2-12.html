Seeing God, Miracles, and Suffering for Service

Why we cannot see the Father physically; how God works miracles; leaders without signs; suffering that prepares for service.

CompleteQuiz 100%
At a glance

Summary

God the Father is Spirit — not limited to a physical form or to time and space. To see God, look at Jesus; in Heaven we shall actually see God. Miracles always have a purpose and do not automatically produce faith in all who see them. John the Baptist was not known for miracle-working powers. Sometimes God allows suffering to prepare a person for special service.

Essay themes

If you preach or review

Why I cannot see God physically — and how I still know Him

I cannot see God physically because God is Spirit; He is not limited to a bodily form or to matter. As the eternal, infinite, and omnipresent Creator, He exists beyond time and space, so there is no physical object for human eyes to see. Scripture calls Him invisible and points us to Jesus, the image of the invisible God. I can still know Him through Christ, Scripture, faith, obedience, and the ways He works in creation and in my life.

Six ways God accomplishes miracles

God accomplishes miracles in six ways: (1) by superseding and transcending all known laws of the universe, as in creation and Christ’s resurrection; (2) by superseding and transcending natural laws, as when He sent a raven to Elijah; (3) by suspending the usual laws of nature; (4) by intensifying His natural laws, as when He changed water into wine; (5) by redirecting natural laws, as in the parting of the Red Sea; and (6) by neutralizing the usual consequences of sin, such as in healing.

Great leaders and prophets without miracle-working fame

Adam, Isaac, Joseph, and David are described as great leaders who performed no recorded miracles. Prophets such as Isaiah and Jeremiah saw God’s work but were not known as miracle workers. John the Baptist also performed no sign, even though Jesus called him the greatest born of women.
