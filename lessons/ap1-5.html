Essay themes

If you preach or review

Image of God (Towns)

Dr. Towns explains that the image of God is not a physical resemblance, not merely humanity’s rational or intellectual ability, and not an unlimited ability to dominate creation. It is rooted in the immaterial nature God gave us. Animals share life and some immaterial abilities, but humans uniquely have personality and intellect. The image includes rational, moral, spiritual, and immortal aspects: we can understand truth, communicate and create, make moral choices, know and relate to God, and continue to exist eternally. Sin corrupted the image, but did not completely erase it.

Theological implications of man's origin (8 points)

1. No independent existence: man is contingent and continues to live by God’s provision, so he is never truly independent of God.
2. Part of creation: although distinct from God, man belongs to the created order.
3. A unique place in creation: being made in God’s image and given dominion sets humanity above the other creatures.
4. Brotherhood among people: all humanity is related through one original pair, so every person has a common human dignity and need.
5. Not the highest object in the universe: human value is great, but worship and ultimate glory belong to God alone.
6. Definite limitations: as finite creatures, we cannot know everything and are subject to error.
7. Proper adjustment in life comes from accepting our finiteness rather than pretending to be God.
8. Man is nonetheless something wonderful: he is God’s expressly designed creation and the highest creature, made in His image.

Three theories of man's origin

The first theory is atheistic evolution: man is viewed as the product of blind, impersonal chemical and biological events, developing through natural processes and survival of the fittest. The second is theistic evolution: God began the creative process by bringing the first organism to life and then allowed evolution to continue, intervening at times to guide it. The third is special creation: man is a direct product of God’s hand, created from the dust and made in God’s image, with Genesis understood as a literal historical account. The biblical evidence best supports God’s direct creative work rather than macro-evolution.
