Lesson 1 · Course 60

Introduction: Luke and the Book of Acts

Luke’s two-volume work — authorship evidence, setting (~60–62), and the kingdom advancing through the gospel.

CompleteQuiz 95%Quiz 1.1 · 90%Quiz 1.2 · 100%Quiz 1.3 · 100%Cumulative Lesson 1 · 95%
At a glance

Summary

Acts is Luke’s second volume to Theophilus (and a wider church), written about 60–62 AD. Early testimony, manuscripts (including Papyrus 75), and we-passages support Lukan authorship even though Luke was not an apostle or eyewitness of Jesus’ earthly ministry. The kingdom expands as God grants salvation through the gospel — including Gentile blessing promised to Abraham.

Big ideas

What to teach and remember

  • Luke–Acts is a two-volume work (Theophilus + Acts as second document).
  • Early writers attribute Luke/Acts to Luke; nobody seriously suggests another author.
  • Evidence includes Anti-Marcionite Prologue, Muratorian Fragment, Papyrus 75, and first-person plural “we” passages.
  • Luke was not an apostle / not an eyewitness of Jesus’ ministry; converted after the ascension; physician; sophisticated Greek.
  • Acts written ~60–62 AD into the Roman Empire’s complex tolerance/oppression of Jews and Christians.
  • Kingdom expands as God grants salvation to believers in Jesus; apostles spread the kingdom by preaching the gospel.
  • OT prophecy: Gentiles blessed through Abraham — Acts records fulfillment.

For the pulpit

Sermon hooks

  • Preach Acts as volume two — same author, same Theophilus, same gospel advancing.
  • The kingdom grows by salvation granted and gospel preached, not by political conquest alone.
