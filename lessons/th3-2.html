Lesson 2 · Course 57

What We Believe About Salvation

Conversion and regeneration meet — human turning and divine new birth, with repentance, justification, adoption, and union with Christ.

CompleteQuiz 100%
At a glance

Summary

The plan of salvation does not begin with knowledge of God’s provision. Conversion is the human side; regeneration is the divine side. You must know both Bible content and the Person of the gospel. Emotional intensity does not make anyone “more saved.” Repentance is a change of mind; justification changes standing; regeneration uses the Spirit as agent and the Bible as instrument; adoption legally introduces sons; Paul’s “together” teaches union with Christ. Noah was not the first justified by faith.

Big ideas

What to teach and remember

  • First step in the plan of salvation is NOT knowledge of God’s provision.
  • Conversion = human side; regeneration = divine side.
  • Must know Bible content (doctrine) and the Person (Jesus Christ) of the gospel to be converted.
  • Emotional experience does not make some people more saved than others.
  • Repentance is essentially a change of mind.
  • Justification changes position/standing before God.
  • Noah was not the first person described as justified by faith.
  • In regeneration: Holy Spirit = agent; Bible = instrument.
  • Adoption is God’s legal introduction of sons into His family.
  • Pauline “together” teaches union with Christ.

Essay themes

If you preach or review

Explain the concept of repentance.

Repentance is a genuine change of mind about sin that results in a change of heart and will. It means turning away from sin and turning toward God in faith and obedience. It is necessary for salvation, but repentance itself does not save; it prepares the sinner to receive Christ, and its reality is shown by a changed life.

List and explain the seven "together" statements in Paul's writings concerning our union with Christ.

Paul describes seven aspects of our union with Christ: (1) crucified together with Christ (Galatians 2:20)—my old life is judged and Christ lives in me; (2) died together with Christ (Colossians 2:20)—I have died to the world’s controlling principles; (3) buried together with Christ (Romans 6:4)—the old life is put away; (4) made alive together with Christ (Ephesians 2:5)—God gives spiritual life by grace; (5) raised together with Christ (Colossians 3:1)—I share His resurrection life and seek the things above; (6) suffer together with Christ (Romans 8:17)—I share His sufferings as an heir; and (7) glorified together with Christ (Romans 8:17)—I will share His future glory.

Outline the four-fold plan of salvation.

The four-fold plan of salvation is: (1) knowledge of the need for salvation—we recognize that all have sinned and fall short of God’s glory; (2) knowledge of the penalty of sin—sin deserves death; (3) knowledge of God’s provision—Christ took the sinner's punishment and died in our place; and (4) a personal response to the good news—we confess Jesus as Lord and believe in Him, receiving salvation by faith.
