The Apostolic Church, Pentecost to 100 AD

The Apostolic Church from Pentecost to AD 100 — Jerusalem Council welcoming Gentiles, Pentecost as Christianity’s foundation and new worship model (preaching, lifestyle, spiritual life), Peter’s Joel 2:28–32 sermon, and the early church’s missionary-pastoral prayer life.

At a glance

Summary

Lesson 4 covers the Apostolic Church from Pentecost to AD 100. The Jerusalem Council welcomed Gentiles into the church. Pentecost is Christianity’s foundation, separating it from its Jewish roots, and established a new worship model centered on the Holy Spirit and Christ — preaching, lifestyle (believers as the temple), and spiritual life — so worship could take place anywhere. Peter’s Pentecost sermon used Joel 2:28–32; about three thousand believed and were baptized. Paul did not attend John’s funeral. The early church prayed with missionary and pastoral focus: when they prayed, things began to happen; prayer is a privilege for all believers; they prayed for sinners’ salvation (not “no need because predetermined”) and for Gospel effectiveness.

Essay themes

If you preach or review

Pentecost worship model

Pentecost brought a new model of worship centered on the Holy Spirit and on Christ rather than on a sacred building or fixed ritual. Preaching the good news became as important as Scripture reading, singing, prayer, and adoration. Worship also meant offering our whole lives to God; believers were now the temple, so a clean and sanctified life mattered. The service was personal, participatory, and congregational, with lay believers sharing in hymns, Scripture, exhortation and teaching, fellowship, breaking bread, and prayer. Because God dwells with His people, worship could take place anywhere.

early church prayer requests — 8

The early church prayed with a missionary and pastoral focus. Eight clear requests were: (1) guidance and discernment for choosing leaders, (2) that the Holy Spirit would work powerfully in believers, (3) the salvation of sinners, (4) that God would send workers into the harvest, (5) the Gospel’s spread and effectiveness, (6) boldness for pastors and missionaries to speak, (7) healing for the sick, and (8) protection and deliverance for His servants. Their prayer was not centered on comfort or buildings, but on God’s will, spiritual power, salvation, the advance of the Gospel, the needs of people, and faithful ministry.

Pentecost Sunday events

On Pentecost Sunday about 120 disciples were gathered in Jerusalem, continuing in prayer as Jesus had commanded. Suddenly there was a sound like a rushing mighty wind that filled the house. Tongues like fire appeared and rested on each person, and all were filled with the Holy Spirit. They began speaking in other languages, so Jewish pilgrims from many nations heard God’s works in their own tongues. Peter explained the event from Joel 2 and proclaimed Jesus. About three thousand people believed, were baptized, and were added to the church. Pentecost therefore marked the Spirit’s empowering presence, the birth of the church’s public witness, and the beginning of an ongoing revival.
