Matthew

Jesus the messianic King — Israel incarnate — fulfilling the promises.

At a glance

Summary

Matthew presents Jesus as the messianic King and “Israel incarnate,” fulfilling OT promises for Jewish readers. Five discourse blocks sit between an introduction and conclusion; the kingdom of heaven has already begun but is not yet complete.

Big ideas

What to teach and remember

  • Jesus is messianic King and “Israel incarnate,” fulfilling OT promises; Matthew quotes the OT more than any other Gospel writer.
  • Kingdom of heaven: already begun, not yet complete (Matt 4:17); “heaven” preferred out of Jewish reverence for the Name.
  • Written especially for Jewish Christians, to convince Jews that Jesus is the Messiah King; expects Jewish customs; aims at Jews across the Mediterranean.
  • Structure: five narrative/discourse blocks marked by “When Jesus had finished saying these things,” plus introduction and conclusion.
  • Most parables center on the kingdom of heaven.
  • Author: Matthew/Levi the tax collector, one of the Twelve; late 60s (temple still standing); earliest manuscripts not Hebrew.
  • Genealogy: legal son of David (often via adoptive parents); “Son of David” used frequently; disciples follow a humble suffering King — not abundant material blessings.
  • Miracles: amazement, then opposition; Olivet Discourse gives hope of future victory; Canaanite woman is the lesson’s case of firm belief.
  • Assembly: Hebrew kahal, Greek ecclesia; “You have heard… but I say” does not nullify the OT law.
Key terms

People, places, and words that matter

Kingdom of heaven

Matthew’s preferred phrase for God’s reign — already begun, not yet complete (4:17); “heaven” shows reverence for the Name.

Israel incarnate

Jesus as the true Israel in a person — fulfilling what Israel was called to be.

Son of David

Royal messianic title Matthew uses more frequently than other names.

Kahal

Hebrew word for assembly — paired with Greek ecclesia.

Ecclesia

Greek word for assembly / church.

Levi

Matthew’s other name — the tax collector among the Twelve.

Canaanite woman

Matthew 15:21–28 — this lesson’s standout case of firm belief.

Olivet Discourse

Jesus’ teaching that gives hope about future victory, not a scare chart.

Structure

Outline / timeline

  1. Introduction (genealogy, birth, beginnings)
  2. Five major sections marked by “When Jesus had finished saying these things”
  3. Conclusion (passion, resurrection, commission)
  4. Within the five: narrative + discourse blocks; parables of the kingdom
  5. Miracles spreading the kingdom → amazement → opposition
  6. Olivet Discourse: hope of future victory

Compare

Side-by-side contrasts

Kingdom already / not yet

  • Already begun (Matt 4:17 — “near”)
  • Not yet completely fulfilled

Assembly words

  • Hebrew — kahal
  • Greek — ecclesia

Early witnesses on Matthew

  • Papias — ordered arrangement of the logia
  • Irenaeus — written Gospel among the Hebrews
  • Tertullian — John and Matthew first instill faith
For the pulpit

Sermon hooks

  • Preach the King who is Israel incarnate — OT promises land on a Person, not a program.
  • Already / not yet: give hope without pretending the kingdom is finished.
  • “You have heard… but I say” fulfills the law; it does not cancel it — careful with antinomian shortcuts.
  • Firm belief looks like the Canaanite woman — keep that story warm for doubtful hearers.
