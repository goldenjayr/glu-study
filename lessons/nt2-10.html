Big ideas

What to teach and remember

  • The authorship of Hebrews is disputed. The lesson does not say everyone knows Paul wrote it. The letter still preaches Christ as better, and the church still reads it as Scripture.
  • Hebrews was written to Jewish Christians involved in Temple worship. A constant temptation existed for them to return to Old Testament ways — so the call is to hold fast to Christ, not go back.
  • Christ is superior: better Son and message, better High Priest, better sacrifice, better covenant, better promises, better mediator. Preach Him; do not send people back to the old system.
  • Six dangers of turning back (not twelve): neglect of so great salvation, unbelief and a hardened heart, disobedience, falling away after much light, willful sin, and refusing God’s voice from heaven.
  • James is the brother of Jesus and a leader of the church in Jerusalem — not John’s brother among the twelve. His letter is practical: real faith shows itself in works and daily conduct.
  • Peter was considered the apostle to the Jews (Paul to the Gentiles) and the leader of the apostles. Some traditions contend that he went to England. Second Peter warns against false teachers; 2 Timothy and 2 Peter share some themes.
  • Peter teaches suffering under persecution: it is not strange; faith is tested like gold; suffer for righteousness without shame; follow Christ’s example; glorify God; commit your soul to the faithful Creator and keep doing good; hope for the coming glory.

Essay themes

If you preach or review

Hebrews' six dangers of returning to the OT

Hebrews gives six serious dangers of turning back from Christ to the Old Testament system: (1) neglecting the great salvation Christ has provided; (2) unbelief and a hardened heart, so a person does not enter God’s rest; (3) failing to grow and falling away after receiving much light; (4) treating Christ’s sacrifice as common and choosing willful sin; (5) drawing back when persecution comes instead of holding fast; (6) refusing God’s voice and grace, which brings His judgment. These warnings show that the old covenant could not save us. Look to Jesus, our perfect High Priest, and continue in faith.

Introductory material of James

James introduces himself as a servant of God and of the Lord Jesus Christ. He writes to the twelve tribes scattered abroad — Jewish believers living in different places. The writer is James, the brother of Jesus and a leader of the church in Jerusalem, not James the brother of John who was one of the twelve. The letter is practical: trials, wisdom, hearing and doing the Word, caring for the poor, controlling the tongue, humility, prayer, and faith that shows itself by works. Real faith should be seen in daily Christian conduct.

Peter's seven teachings on suffering during persecution

Peter’s seven teachings for believers who suffer during persecution: (1) suffering for Christ is not strange — it can be part of the Christian calling; (2) trials test and prove the genuineness of faith like fire tests gold; (3) suffering for righteousness is a blessing, not a reason for shame; (4) follow Christ’s example — He suffered without sin and did not answer evil with evil; (5) do not be ashamed to bear Christ’s name, but glorify God in it; (6) commit your souls to the faithful Creator and keep doing what is right; (7) rejoice in hope, trust God’s care, and look for the coming glory — God will judge rightly and restore His people. Stay humble, faithful, and prayerful instead of giving up.
