Lesson 3 · Course 60

Pentecost, Apostles, and Church Leadership

Spirit power at Pentecost, what makes an apostle, and how the church learns to lead and delegate.

CompleteQuiz 95%Quiz 3.1 · 100%Quiz 3.2 · 100%Quiz 3.3 · 100%Cumulative Lesson 3 · 95%
At a glance

Summary

Pentecost brings Spirit power for God’s will, miracles, and witness — tongues as known human languages — and begins a great harvest of salvation under Jeremiah’s new-covenant promise. Apostolic identity is tightly defined (risen Lord, divine appointment, taught by Jesus); there are no apostles today in that foundational sense. Church leadership includes elders/deacons without universal agreement on the offices, and the Jerusalem Council was not apostles-only.

Big ideas

What to teach and remember

  • OT Spirit power: bring God’s will to pass, miracles, witness.
  • Pentecost tongues = unknown human languages; begins great harvest of salvation.
  • Jeremiah: new covenant — law in minds and hearts.
  • Kingdom comes as a long process in stages (not all at once).
  • Apostolos = one who is sent; marks include signs/wonders/miracles; requirements: seen risen Lord, appointed by God, taught by Jesus.
  • No apostles today — unique foundational role.
  • Gospel channels: public speeches, dialogue, households, personal evangelism.
  • Jerusalem Council not apostles-only; Acts 6 = daily food distribution; apostles/prophets = church foundation.
  • Enter the kingdom through many hardships; false teachers would arise from Ephesians.
  • Cumul miss: James was not the Jerusalem church leader (Moodle False).

Compare

Side-by-side contrasts

Kingdom timing

  • Not: Messiah all at once and suddenly
  • Yes: long process in stages

Apostles then vs now

  • Then: seen risen Lord, appointed by God, taught by Jesus; signs/wonders
  • Now: no longer have apostles in that foundational sense
For the pulpit

Sermon hooks

  • Preach Pentecost as harvest beginning under a new-covenant heart, not a circus of phenomena.
  • Keep apostleship historical and foundational — Christ still sends workers without reinventing the Twelve.
  • Delegation in Acts 6 protects the Word and the widows.
  • Flag the miss: James’s death wakes the church; he is not framed as Jerusalem’s standing leader on that item.
