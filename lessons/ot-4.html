Joshua and Judges

Victory and fulfillment in Joshua; the cycle of compromise in Judges; Ruth’s redemption.

CompleteQuiz 100%
At a glance

Summary

Victory and fulfillment in Joshua; the cycle of compromise in Judges; Ruth’s redemption. Joshua’s theme is fulfillment and victory — God keeping the land-promise. Judges’ theme is the warning against compromise. Do not swap the banners.

CompleteQuiz 100%
Big ideas

What to teach and remember

  • Joshua’s theme is fulfillment and victory — God keeping the land-promise. Judges’ theme is the warning against compromise. Do not swap the banners.
  • Rahab is spared. Faith can hang a scarlet cord in a collapsing city. The gospel has always made room for unlikely family.
  • Caleb asks for Hebron at eighty-five. Age is not a disqualification when the promise still stands.
  • The judges are not stained-glass saints. God delivers through imperfect people — and the refrain “everyone did what was right in his own eyes” still preaches.
  • Ruth teaches the doctrine of redemption: a kinsman who can, who will, and who pays. Boaz is a window; Christ is the door.
  • Incomplete obedience in Joshua becomes next generation’s snare. Finish the work God actually assigned.

Quiz drill
Quiz drill / review · open to practice · 10 items

Question wording reconstructed from session notes.

Quiz score: 100% · 1 attempt

1. The theme of Joshua is a warning against compromise.

FalseThat warning belongs to Judges. Joshua is fulfillment and victory.

2. Israel entered a death pact of capital punishment.

TrueJoshua 1:18 — whoever rebels against the command shall be put to death. The conquest is not casual.

3. Rahab was executed by stoning.

FalseRahab was spared. Faith brought her into Israel’s story — and into Messiah’s line.

4. The altar on Mount Ebal was built of precision-cut stone.

FalseIt was uncut stones. Worship is received, not manufactured to our taste.

5. Caleb conquered Hebron at the age of 85.

TrueA whole heart at eighty-five still takes mountains.

6. The author of Judges was Moses.

FalseSamuel is identified as the author, not Moses.

7. The judges were all men of uncompromising character.

FalseThe book’s theme includes their imperfections. God uses cracked vessels.

8. Gideon was the most inferior of his father’s house.

TrueGod delights to save through the least, so the glory stays His.

9. Samson was a Nazarite.

TrueSet apart from the womb — and still a warning about wasted strength.

10. The book of Ruth teaches the doctrine of redemption.

TrueThe kinsman-redeemer is the pastoral heart of the story.
Essay themes

If you preach or review

Ai

Jericho’s victory, then Ai’s defeat — hidden sin in the camp, overconfidence, and a God who will not be used. Restoration comes through confession and a second, obedient battle.

Gideon

The least in his house, too many soldiers, a fleece, and a God who reduces the army so Israel cannot boast. Courage is not the absence of fear; it is obedience with trembling.

Ruth, Boaz, and the kinsman-redeemer

Nearness, ability, willingness, and the public price. Preach redemption as personal and costly — then name the greater Boaz.
