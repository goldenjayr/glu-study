Compare

Side-by-side contrasts

Five arguments — what each starts from

  • Cosmological: chain of causes/effects → First Cause / Uncaused Cause / Prime Mover (something ≠ nothing)
  • Teleological: order, harmony, purposeful design → intelligent Designer (watch → watchmaker)
  • Anthropological (moral): human intellect, freedom, conscience, moral awareness → personal God
  • Ontological: concept of a perfect/greatest Being → that Being exists (mind can’t conceive the non-existent)
  • Congruity: creation + design + human nature + reason + revelation together → best fit is God’s existence

Logical arguments — save vs condemn

  • NOT sufficient to bring salvation
  • ARE sufficient to condemn a person who will not respond to God

Essay themes

If you preach or review

five arguments for God's existence

1. Cosmological: The world is a chain of causes and effects, so it points to a First Cause or Uncaused Cause—God.
2. Teleological: The order, harmony, and purposeful design in the universe point to an intelligent Designer.
3. Anthropological (moral): Human intellect, freedom, conscience, and moral awareness are best explained by a personal God who is the source of our nature and moral standard.
4. Ontological: Our concept of a perfect and greatest Being points to the existence of that Being, since the mind cannot conceive of what does not exist.
5. Congruity: When all the facts—creation, design, human nature, reason, and revelation—are considered together, they fit best with the existence of God.

Cosmological Argument

The Cosmological Argument reasons from the existence of the world to a First Cause. Everything that begins or exists as an effect depends on a cause, and something cannot come from nothing. Creation exists, so it cannot explain itself; the chain of causes cannot regress endlessly without a beginning. Therefore, the universe must ultimately trace back to an uncaused, independent, all-powerful First Cause, also called the Uncaused Cause or Prime Mover. This Cause must be free, not forced by necessity, because it brought the world into existence at a point in time. It must also have intelligence and will: a timeless Cause could begin a temporal series only by freely choosing to create. Thus the order and existence of creation point beyond themselves to God as the Creator.

God's self-revelation from within man / conscience

God's self-revelation from within man is seen especially in conscience and moral awareness. Romans 2:15 says that even Gentiles who do not have the written law show that its work is written on their hearts, while their consciences accuse or excuse them. Because people are made in God's image, they naturally recognize moral absolutes and feel accountable when they do right or wrong. Laws in society also imply a Lawgiver and Judge, and our impulse to judge wrongdoing reflects this inner moral knowledge. Although sin has damaged the image of God in us, it has not erased it. This inward witness points to a personal, holy, and moral God and supports the truth of Scripture.
