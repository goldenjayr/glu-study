New Attacks on Christianity from Postmodernism

Modernism emptied the well; postmodernism reacted — and the church was not ready.

CompleteQuiz 100%
At a glance

Summary

We live in a culture that is no longer Christian. Modernism treated Scripture with a liberal/humanist approach; postmodernism rose as a reaction to modernism’s emptiness. The church was not well prepared. Towns’s best response: present the true church — communicate the gospel without compromise, and distinguish cultural from hostile academic postmodernism.

Essay themes

If you preach or review

List and discuss the five points of scientific demonstration of truth.

The five points move from observation to a tested conclusion:

1. Gather information concerning a problem. We begin by collecting the relevant facts rather than assuming an answer.

2. Analyze the facts and data. The information has to be examined carefully so that important evidence is not overlooked.

3. Create a hypothesis—a proposed or suggested solution to the problem.

4. Test the proposed solution. The lesson mentions testing its logic, correspondence to the reality of life, analysis of the data, consistency, comprehensiveness (whether relevant questions have been answered), and public acceptance or consensus.

5. Announce a solution, principle, or law. Only after the proposed answer has been examined and tested should it be presented as a conclusion.

List and discuss the ten presuppositions of modernism.

1. Naturalism: reality is restricted to what can be observed or proved.
2. Humanism: humanity is viewed as the pinnacle of the universe.
3. The scientific method: knowledge is inherently good and attainable through a reliable method.
4. Reductionism: humans are understood as highly developed animals.
5. Progress: because knowledge is good, gaining more knowledge is expected to produce progress.
6. Nature: evolution, rather than a Creator, is held responsible for life and its development.
7. Certainty: because knowledge is objective, people can know things with certainty.
8. Determinism: events happen because of fixed causes.
9. Individualism: the individual and the individual's ability to discern truth are supreme.
10. Anti-authoritarianism: each person is the final arbiter of truth.

Together these assumptions place confidence in observable reality, human reason, progress, and individual judgment rather than in God's revealed authority.

List and discuss the seven cultural expressions of postmodernism.

The seven cultural expressions of postmodernism are:

1. Denial of personal objectivity. People say that their beliefs are shaped by their parents or background and that nobody can know for sure.
2. Uncertainty of knowledge. Even when a government or expert makes a claim, the response is often, "Who really knows if it is true?"
3. Death of any all-inclusive explanation. People resist neat explanations and believe that life does not fit one complete, orderly account.
4. Denial of knowledge's inherent goodness. More knowledge and technology are seen as potentially making the world more dangerous, not automatically better.
5. Rejection of progress. Having technology and material advancement does not guarantee happiness or fulfillment.
6. Supremacy of community-based knowledge. It is considered arrogant for one individual to claim that he or she alone has discovered spiritual truth; knowledge is shaped by one's community.
7. Disbelief in objective inquiry. Even when reading Scripture, a person may say, "This is what I think it means, but I could be wrong—what is your interpretation?"

In everyday life these ideas are usually expressed as attitudes rather than as a formal philosophy. They show why Christians need humility and patient conversation while still presenting the objective truth of the gospel.
