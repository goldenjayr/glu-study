Essay themes

If you preach or review

Five views of Spirit baptism

The lesson presents five main views. First, the Wesleyan view treats Spirit baptism as a second work of grace after salvation, setting the believer apart for holy living and, in its strongest form, removing the sin nature. Second, the Keswick view, associated with J. R. Rice and R. A. Torrey, also sees a distinct post-conversion work that gives the believer power for holy living and service. Third, the Scofield Reference Bible view says that at salvation the Holy Spirit places the believer positionally into the universal church, the body of Christ. Fourth, the local-church or water-baptism view says that Spirit baptism takes place when a believer is placed into a local church through water baptism. Fifth, the Calvary or atonement view sees Spirit baptism as identification with Christ’s body on the cross, including His death, burial, and resurrection.

Seven words/titles describing a pastor

As an elder, the pastor must be spiritually mature and provide wise rule and care for the church. As a bishop, he is an overseer who carries chief responsibility for the church’s spiritual and administrative direction, without becoming authoritarian. As a pastor or shepherd, he cares for, feeds, guides, corrects, and protects Christ’s flock. As a preacher, he heralds God’s Word so that people are educated, exhorted, and comforted. As a teacher, he explains Scripture and feeds the flock with sound doctrine. As a servant, he leads humbly by serving the people rather than seeking status or control. As a messenger, he faithfully receives and delivers God’s message to the church. These titles describe one pastoral ministry from different angles: mature leadership, oversight, shepherding, proclamation, teaching, humble service, and faithful communication.
